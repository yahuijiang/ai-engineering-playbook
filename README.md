# AI Engineering Playbook

A practical reference for how AI is integrated into real-world software engineering practices and SDLC workflows.

This repository is not about AI tools. It focuses on how engineering organizations can use AI in a consistent, scalable, and governable way — beyond individual productivity use cases.

---

## Context

AI coding tools such as Copilot, Claude Code, and Cursor are now widely adopted across engineering teams. In most organizations, however, AI usage remains primarily individual:

- Developers use AI tools independently
- Prompting practices vary significantly across teams
- Context is not consistently shared or standardized
- Engineering workflows remain largely unchanged
- AI-generated outputs are difficult to evaluate and govern

The result is local productivity gains, but limited impact on end-to-end software delivery.

---

## Key Question

The real question is not which AI tool to use. It is:

> How do we integrate AI into the way software is designed, built, reviewed, and delivered?

---

## Engineering Perspective

AI adoption in software engineering can be understood across four levels:

| Level              | Focus |
|--------------------|------|
| Tool               | Individual usage (faster coding, assistance) |
| Workflow           | Team collaboration in daily engineering work |
| SDLC               | End-to-end delivery processes |
| Engineering System | Standards, governance, and operating models |

This playbook focuses on the layers above the tools — workflows, SDLC, and engineering systems.

> AI should be embedded into the engineering system, not treated as an isolated productivity tool.

---

## Key Areas

The playbook is organized around four parallel concerns:

### 1. AI-Native Development Workflows

Engineering workflows designed to incorporate AI throughout the development lifecycle — including Specification-Driven Development, Agent-Assisted Development, AI-Augmented Code Review, AI-Assisted Testing, and AI-Assisted Documentation.

### 2. Standards and Practices

Shared standards that enable teams to collaborate effectively with AI — including Prompt Standards, Context Engineering, Documentation Standards, Review Standards, and Knowledge Management Practices.

### 3. Governance and Compliance

Mechanisms that ensure AI is used safely and responsibly in enterprise environments — including AI Usage Policies, Human Oversight Models, Auditability, Compliance Requirements, and Risk Management Practices.

### 4. Measurement and Continuous Improvement

Methods for evaluating whether AI adoption produces meaningful engineering outcomes — including Engineering Productivity Metrics, Workflow Adoption Metrics, AI Contribution Tracking, Quality Indicators, and SDLC Performance Improvements.
